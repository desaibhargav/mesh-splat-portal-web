# Saved views and asset orientation

The shared **Views** tool saves a non-destructive asset orientation and camera arrangement. The world grid stays fixed. PlayCanvas's rotation gizmo rotates the displayed mesh or splat; the native `ViewCube` widget changes only the camera between six world directions. Axis labels follow PlayCanvas's world coordinates, with Y up. They do not infer an asset's semantic top or front.

## Workflow

1. Open Views. Existing entries appear in a separate saved-views box; click one to restore it for editing, or choose **Create view**.
2. Enter **View name** first. The rotation gizmo and fixed world grid appear immediately for the draft. Drag the native rings to orient the asset; orbit outside them or use Alt/Option/right-drag, pan with middle-drag, and scroll to zoom. There is no Move mode in this workflow; the earlier W/E reference-plane controls have been replaced by asset rotation.
3. Optionally choose **Align to three points**. Pick three visible surfaces: an origin, an X direction, and a third point establishing the plane. The resulting frame is rotated into world XY, with its normal toward world +Z. The normal sign follows the current camera before alignment so click winding is predictable. Rotation remains about the asset pivot; this operation does not translate, scale, or calibrate geometry. Repeated or collinear points are rejected. Cancel or Q aborts placement.
4. **Reset orientation** restores the imported presentation orientation (including the existing splat import convention). The current camera is the default view; saving captures the camera currently displayed, so no separate capture button is needed. The viewer-wide **Four views** workspace provides orthographic inspection alongside Perspective; its axis widgets choose the six world directions.
5. **Save view** creates an entry; **Update view** updates the entry being edited. Both record asset rotation, camera origin/orbit target, orientation, distance, FOV, projection, and orthographic height. **Cancel** or leaving the tool with an unsaved draft restores the previous arrangement. Saving commits the arrangement for continued use of other tools. The minus button deletes an entry.
6. In [Cross section](sectioning.md), choosing a saved view restores its asset orientation and camera. Choose the cutting-plane normal there: the saved viewing direction or world X/Y/Z, then offset/tilt/flip it independently of the asset.

Views also appear in the model tree, where they can be restored for editing or renamed. See [four-view workspace](viewports.md) for shared panel navigation and tool composition.

Views remain session-local and disappear when leaving or refreshing the asset. They do not duplicate or overwrite model geometry, selections, colors, measurements, or annotations. Complete working-document persistence/export remains deferred.

## Shared coordinate space and engine reuse

`ViewerSpace` owns one native parent `Entity` for the asset. It applies a rigid rotation about the asset's initial bounding-box center using native `Quat`, `Vec3`, and scene transforms. Asset child transforms, source buffers, and primitive IDs stay unchanged. Point tools store anchors in that stable presentation-local space. Annotation entities inherit the parent transform; measurement visuals transform their existing endpoints to world coordinates using reused vectors. Distances and undo snapshots stay valid across rotation and recall. Picking and selection bounds continue to use the engine's actual world transforms. Orientation changes invalidate picker caches without rebuilding geometry or uploading masks.

`RotateGizmo` supplies ring geometry, picking, and manipulation. A small helper at the stable pivot forwards its rotation into `ViewerSpace`. A fixed grid uses native `drawLines`; its ten subdivisions per side are display policy and its extent derives from the asset bounds. There are no asset vertex traversals or extra model/camera render passes for orientation. Helper layers and resources are released on tool exit or application destruction; the camera's previous enabled state is restored after native drags and cancellation.

The engine `ViewCube` is a DOM/SVG axis widget, not a literal cube with labeled faces. Its six direction events call the same camera-pose operations; accessible labels identify the world viewpoints. Its SVG is updated only when the camera matrix changes. The parent-space and scene-gizmo operations can be reused by XR inputs; the DOM widget/panels and mouse bindings require immersive presentation/input work. Desktop orthographic inspection does not replace headset eye projections. No headset certification is claimed.

The bundled `CameraControls` script in PlayCanvas 2.21.4 loses roll through `Pose.look` in its reset/focus APIs. Our pinned subclass's `restorePose` synchronizes the engine's `Pose`, active `InputController`, camera entity, and focus distance on recall. Its script name is explicit so production minification cannot change registration. The native controller changes orbit distance for wheel/pinch zoom; the subclass also applies that distance ratio to orthographic height, outside XR sessions. Focus uses an eight-corner bounds projection for orthographic framing. Review the protected-field boundary on engine upgrades.

## Verification

Development and minified production browser checks on a real mesh and splat exercise asset rotation with a fixed camera, attached annotation anchors, unchanged measurement distances, create/edit/update/cancel, six-direction navigation, orthographic recall, section-view loading and world-axis cutting, Q/navigation, mobile panel bounds, and teardown. Production checks also complete three-point alignment on both formats. Focused unit tests cover rigid transforms, stable pivot, inverse anchor conversion, unchanged asset child transforms, distance preservation, bounds, three-point degeneracy/scale, and native camera pose restoration. Follow [browser verification](browser-verification.md); temporary harnesses and derivative fixtures remain outside the repository. These checks do not establish Safari, WebGPU, or immersive-session compatibility.
